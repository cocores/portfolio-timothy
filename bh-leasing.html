B&H Photo Video serves 14 million visitors a year — professional photographers, videographers, and business buyers spending thousands per order. The leasing program was a real competitive advantage. It just wasn't discoverable. This is the story of how I fixed that.

The Context

B&H is one of the largest photo and video retailers in the US. A significant share of its revenue comes from high-consideration purchases — cameras, lenses, studio lighting, broadcast equipment — where a $5,000–$50,000 order is routine for professional and business buyers.

For that segment, financing isn't a nice-to-have. It's how they manage cash flow, preserve capital, and move fast on equipment decisions. B&H had a leasing program with competitive terms. The problem was structural: customers had to already know it existed to find it.

"This wasn't a demand problem. It was a distribution problem. The program was real — the path to it wasn't."

What I Found

When I dug into the data and talked to internal stakeholders, the picture came into focus quickly. The leasing program wasn't underperforming because customers didn't want financing. It was underperforming because the discovery path was broken at every level.

  • No PDP placement. The highest-intent surface on the site — the product page — had no financing CTA. Customers at the moment of decision had no signal that leasing was an option.
  • Fragmented entry points. Financing information was scattered across multiple pages with inconsistent messaging and no clear "apply now" path.
  • Low internal awareness. Sales and customer support teams couldn't confidently explain the program, so they weren't surfacing it proactively.
  • Multi-step drop-off. Customers who did find the program encountered a friction-heavy application flow that lost them before completion.

The key insight: Every customer buying a $10K camera setup has already decided they want the gear. The question is how they want to pay for it. We were answering that question too late — or not at all.

What I Did

My approach was to collapse the discovery journey. Instead of routing customers through a separate financing hub, I brought the leasing program to the Product Detail Page — where the purchase decision is already happening.

PDP Integration

I led the discovery, prioritization, and execution to surface leasing as a primary payment option directly on the PDP — alongside standard checkout. The placement was contextual: visible at the right moment in the purchase flow, not buried in a tab or footer link.

Centralized Application Path

The fragmented entry points were consolidated into a single, streamlined application flow. I worked with engineering and design to reduce friction at every step — cutting the number of screens, clarifying lender options, and removing the dead-ends that were killing completion rates.

Internal Enablement

I worked with the sales and support org to build a consistent internal reference for the program — what it offers, who qualifies, and how to recommend it. If internal teams aren't confident in a product, customers won't hear about it in the moments that matter.

Measurement Framework

I defined the funnel from PDP visit through submitted application, and built tracking to see exactly where drop-off was happening. That data drove ongoing iteration — we kept optimizing the flow as adoption grew.

The Funnel

The Result

Application volume increased by 225%. That single number tells the story — this wasn't a small optimization. Moving financing to the PDP changed who could even consider it.

The revenue impact — $11.1M in new annual revenue — came entirely from an existing program. No new product. No new budget. No new lender relationships. Just better placement and a path customers could actually follow.

What I Took Away

  • Distribution beats product quality in mature categories. The leasing program was already competitive. The bottleneck was surface area, not substance.
  • The right surface matters more than the right message. We didn't rewrite the value proposition — we moved it to the place where it was actually relevant.
  • Internal teams are a growth channel. Sales and support talking confidently about financing created a second discovery path that analytics alone wouldn't show.
  • Funnel visibility is a strategy input, not just a reporting tool. Tracking from PDP to submission let us prioritize what to fix next and show the business why each change mattered.
