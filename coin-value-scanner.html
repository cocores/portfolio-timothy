The Problem

Whether you're at a coin show, a flea market, or sorting through a collection, the same question always comes up: what is this actually worth? For slabbed coins, the PCGS grade is right there on the label — but the current market value isn't. For raw (unslabbed) coins, you're estimating grade by eye before you can even start looking.

The current workflow: take a photo, open a browser, navigate to PCGS or a similar registry, type in the coin details, find the right grade row, cross-reference recent auction data. That's a three-minute process for something that should take three seconds.

"The grade is right on the label. The value shouldn't require a laptop to find."

The Idea

Coin Value Scanner uses your camera to identify the coin and pull its current PCGS market value — for both slabbed coins (where the grade is already certified on the label) and raw coins (where the app estimates grade from the image). No typing, no lookup tables, no browser tabs.

It's built for the moment of decision: you're holding a coin and you need to know if the price is right — right now.

How It Works

Point Your Camera

Open the app and point your camera at any coin — slabbed in its PCGS holder or raw in your hand. The scanner reads the coin's details and grade information from the label or estimates grade from the coin's surface.

Coin Is Identified

Computer vision identifies the coin type, year, mint mark, and — for slabbed coins — reads the certified PCGS grade directly from the label. For raw coins, the app provides a grade range estimate based on visible surface quality.

Get the Market Value

The app pulls the current PCGS population report data and price guide value for that coin and grade. You see the market value, recent auction comparables, and population data — how many coins exist at that grade and above.

Save or Share

Add scanned coins to your personal collection tracker, export a value summary, or share a coin's details and current value with another collector. Your scanned history stays saved for future reference.

What I'm Exploring

  • Label OCR accuracy — reliably reading PCGS grade labels across different lighting conditions and holder types
  • Raw coin grading — using computer vision to estimate grade on unslabbed coins by surface and luster
  • PCGS data integration — connecting to population report and price guide data in real time
  • Collection tracker — letting users build and value their full collection over time
  • Auction comparables — surfacing recent sales data alongside the price guide value
  • Offline mode — core lookups available without a connection for use at shows and markets
