The Problem

You're in Midtown. You've been walking for an hour. You need a bathroom. You know every Starbucks within a half-mile has a code on the door — but you don't know the code. So you buy a $7 coffee you didn't want just to get access to a room that was always there.

This happens everywhere, every day. And the information exists — it's just locked in the heads of people who've been there before, or hidden on some forgotten Reddit thread from 2019.

"Ace Hotel Midtown's bathroom code is 1524. Now you know. Pass it on."

The Idea

Bathroom Codes is a community-maintained map of bathroom access information. Users submit codes, confirm existing ones, flag outdated entries, and earn trust over time. The more people contribute, the more useful it gets for everyone.

It's a classic network-effects problem with a very clear value exchange: share what you know, get access to what everyone else knows.

The App

How It Works

Submit What You Know

Anyone can submit a bathroom location with a code, notes on accessibility, and a rough cleanliness rating. Takes 30 seconds. The entry goes live after basic moderation and community confirmation.

Community Confirms It

Other users who visit can confirm the code still works, flag it as changed, or add updated information. Entries decay in trust score over time unless regularly confirmed — keeping the data fresh.

Search When You Need It

Open the app, see what's nearby, get the code. Sorted by distance, trust score, and last confirmed date. No account required to look up a code — just to submit one.

Earn Trust Over Time

Contributors who submit accurate, regularly confirmed entries build reputation in the community. Trusted submitters get early access to new cities and features as the product grows.

In Development
Actively building — data model, submission flow, and map UX underway

What I'm Exploring

  • Data freshness model — how entries age, decay, and get revived through community confirmation
  • Trust and reputation — incentivizing accurate submissions without gamifying it awkwardly
  • Moderation at scale — keeping entries clean without a full-time team watching submissions
  • Anonymous vs. account — the right balance between frictionless lookup and community contribution
  • City expansion — starting dense (NYC) and working outward as community density allows
  • Partnerships — hotels, coffee chains, and co-working spaces as verified contributors
