Case Studies

How I think through hard calls

Ecommerce · B&H Photo Video · 2023–Present

Leasing existed — but nobody could find it. Do we fix discoverability or redesign the whole experience?

Problem B&H offered equipment leasing for professionals, but it was scattered across the site, inconsistently surfaced, and rarely visible at the moment a customer was actually deciding to buy.
Options A) Surface leasing links more prominently on existing pages — low lift, low impact. B) Centralize the entire leasing experience on the PDP as a first-class purchase option alongside buy and rent.
Decision Chose B. The data showed high-consideration buyers made financing decisions at the product level, not after clicking through menus. The PDP was the right moment — we just weren't showing up there.
Outcome 225% increase in leasing application submissions and $11.1M in additional revenue. We didn't invent leasing — we just gave it a home.
Collaborative Design · InVision · 2018–2020

When the roadmap is overloaded, how do you decide what to cut — features or speed?

Problem Freehand was competing in a fast-moving market. The team faced constant pressure to ship — but cramming the roadmap with features risked shipping things half-baked and losing credibility with design-obsessed users.
Options A) Ship fast and broad — match competitors feature-for-feature, iterate on feedback. B) Ship fewer things, but with greater depth and polish — protect the product's reputation for quality.
Decision Made deliberate trade-offs between features and speed-to-market based on user behavior data and stakeholder alignment. Defined what "done" meant before writing a single story, not after.
Outcome Continuous improvement across Freehand with a track record of shipping well, not just shipping fast. Post-launch metrics became the input loop for the next cycle — not an afterthought.
SaaS · InternetBrands Demandforce · 2020–2022

User engagement is dropping. Do you optimize the existing flow or go back to first principles?

Problem Key engagement and retention metrics were declining. The pressure was to patch the funnel — add tooltips, tweak copy, run a quick A/B test — and ship something fast to show progress.
Options A) Optimize the existing flow with targeted fixes based on where users were dropping. B) Conduct user research first, reframe the problem, and build the roadmap around what customers actually needed.
Decision Chose B. Defined the product roadmap based on customer feedback and market trends, not instinct. Created detailed user stories and ran user acceptance testing before anything went to dev.
Outcome Improved user engagement and retention metrics. Go-to-market strategy developed in lockstep with marketing and sales drove meaningful adoption growth — not just a one-week spike.
