Independent pet stores are a hard vertical to digitize. Owners know their customers by name and carry inventory Chewy doesn't stock — but they have no online presence and no way to reach pet owners searching the web. Promenade sat in between, and my job was to make both sides of that marketplace work.

The Problem

Promenade was entering the pet retail vertical with a cold-start problem on both sides of the marketplace. Independent pet store owners were signing up, but the path from "signed up" to "making sales" was broken. Onboarding took days. Product catalogs — often hundreds of SKUs across food, supplies, and specialty items — were incomplete or never published. And without a working supply side, there was nothing compelling to show buyers.

The demand side had its own friction: pet owners who preferred shopping local had no reliable way to find or trust an independent store online. They couldn't see what was in stock, didn't know if the store was active, and had no signal to distinguish a specialty shop with knowledgeable staff from a ghost listing.

"Pet store owners aren't tech-averse — they're time-starved. Our job was to make going digital feel faster than it actually was."

What I Found

I mapped the full pet store owner journey — from signup through first online sale — and the drop-off clustered at the same predictable moments every time.

  • Catalog setup was the biggest barrier. Pet stores carry wide, complex inventory — multiple brands, sizes, and formulations of food alone. The setup flow asked for too much at once, and owners were abandoning before publishing anything.
  • Time to first sale was too long. Store owners who finished onboarding but didn't see a sale within two weeks churned at 3x the rate of those who did. They needed early proof that the platform would work for them.
  • Buyers couldn't evaluate stores they didn't know. Shop pages had no inventory freshness signal, no indication of local pickup or same-day availability, and no way to see the store's specialization — the exact things a pet owner uses to decide whether to try a new shop.
  • No visibility into what was working. Store owners had no analytics on which products were getting viewed or purchased, so they couldn't improve their listings and had no reason to stay engaged between sales.

The core insight: Pet store owners didn't need a complex e-commerce platform. They needed a fast path to their first online order — proof that the channel was worth maintaining. Every decision I made optimized for shortening that window.

Strategy & Alignment

I framed the entire roadmap around a single metric: time-to-first-sale. That gave the team a shared north star connecting supply-side activation to buyer-side demand — and made it easy to say no. Work that shortened the activation window moved forward. Work that didn't had to wait.

Cross-functionally, this required alignment from three teams: engineering (onboarding redesign), operations (a merchant success playbook built for pet store owners specifically), and growth (demand-gen targeting pet owners near active stores). I ran a discovery sprint with each before writing a line of code to surface dependencies early and set shared OKRs.

What I Built

Streamlined Pet Store Onboarding

I redesigned onboarding around a minimum viable catalog — the smallest set of products that would get a store to their first sale. Rather than importing full inventory upfront, we guided owners to publish their top five bestsellers in under 20 minutes, with a live preview of how the shop would look to buyers. Completion rate on onboarding increased significantly in the first month after launch.

Buyer Trust Layer

I shipped a store-page trust layer tailored to the pet vertical: last-active timestamp, local pickup availability, same-day fulfillment flag, and a verification badge for stores that had passed our catalog quality bar. Pet owners buying food or medication for their animals have a high bar for trust — these signals gave them a reason to try a store they'd never ordered from before.

Store Performance Dashboard

I worked with engineering to ship a lightweight analytics view for store owners: which products were getting views, which were converting, and what the top performers had in common. This gave engaged owners a reason to keep their listings current — and gave our merchant success team a data layer to run proactive outreach from when a store went quiet.

Local Discovery & Search Improvements

I partnered with growth to improve how active, high-quality pet stores surfaced in search and category pages. Inventory freshness, catalog completeness, and fulfillment speed fed a ranking signal that put the best local stores at the top — and gave owners a direct incentive to keep their pages current.

The Activation Funnel

Every improvement mapped back to the same funnel. Pet store owners who hit their first sale within two weeks stayed on the platform. Those who didn't, churned. Compressing that window was the whole game.

The Outcome

Within the year, the pet store marketplace reached $5M ARR. Store GMV grew 3x on the back of better activation and higher catalog quality. Time-to-first-sale dropped by 40%, which compressed the churn window and kept more store owners on the platform long enough to see real, recurring revenue from online orders.

The work also created a compounding flywheel: better-equipped stores with fresher inventory meant a better buyer experience, which drove more local pet owners to the platform, which gave store owners more reason to stay active. That loop — not any single feature — was what turned a cold-start pet retail marketplace into a working one.

What I Took Away

  • Vertical specificity unlocks activation. Generic merchant onboarding didn't work for pet store owners — their catalog complexity (food formulations, breed-specific products, live goods) required a tailored minimum viable catalog approach. Understanding the vertical deeply made the difference between onboarding that stalled and onboarding that converted.
  • Time-to-value is a retention strategy. Getting store owners to a first online sale fast wasn't just a growth metric — it was the most predictive signal of whether they'd stay. The two-week window was narrow and we had to design explicitly to close it.
  • Trust signals are vertical-dependent. Pet owners buying food or medication needed different trust cues than a general consumer. Same-day availability and local pickup mattered more than review counts. Shipping the right signals — not the default ones — moved conversion.
  • Data visibility changes behavior. Store owners who could see which products were performing kept their catalogs fresher and published more SKUs. A lightweight analytics view cost little to build and compounded over time as owners optimized around what they saw.
