Wingd
Friend-approved dating

Modern dating apps have an accountability problem. You're matched with algorithmic strangers, and there's no social context — no shared history, no trust signal, no one who actually knows you vouching for the other person. Wingd fixes that by making your friends part of the match.

The Problem

Swipe-based dating is fast and abundant, but it's also anonymous and low-stakes in the worst way. Anyone can match with anyone. There's no friction that signals real intent. The result is high volume, low quality, and a lot of ghosting.

Meanwhile, people who meet through mutual friends or social circles tend to have better outcomes — more context, more trust, more reason to show up. The problem is that this kind of social infrastructure doesn't scale. You can only have so many mutual friends.

"The best relationships start with someone who already knows both of you saying — hey, you two should meet."

The Idea

Wingd brings that dynamic into an app. Before you can match and chat with someone, at least one person in your approved inner circle has to vouch for the connection. They see a brief profile, give a thumbs up or down, and optionally leave a note.

This does a few things at once:

  • Adds accountability. You're not just swiping anonymously — people you trust are part of the loop.
  • Creates signal. A match that your friend approved means something. It's not algorithmic — it's personal.
  • Makes it social. Your friends are invested. They'll ask how it went. That changes the dynamic.
  • Reduces noise. Fewer matches, but higher quality. The friction is the feature.

How It Works

Build Your Wing Circle

When you join, you invite 2–5 close friends to be your "wings." These are the people who know you well enough to say — yeah, this person is worth your time.

Browse and Signal Interest

You browse profiles and mark people you're interested in. Instead of an instant match, your interest goes into a queue for your wings to review.

Your Wings Weigh In

Your wing circle sees the profile, votes, and optionally adds a quick note. If a majority approves, the interest is sent. The other person goes through the same process on their end.

A Match With Context

When both sides get wing approval, you're matched. You can see how many friends vouched for the connection and any notes they left. The conversation starts with context, not cold.

What I'm Exploring

  • Core match flow — the friend approval queue, voting UX, and match reveal experience
  • Wing dynamics — how many wings is right, how to keep them engaged over time
  • Notification design — nudging wings without making them feel like they're doing work
  • Privacy model — what your wings see, what they don't, and how to make both sides comfortable
  • Growth loop — the invite structure as the primary acquisition channel
